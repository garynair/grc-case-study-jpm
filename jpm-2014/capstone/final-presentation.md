# Capstone: Final Team Presentation

The capstone has two parts:

1. **[Task 9 — Incident tabletop and executive briefing](task-09-conduct-the-incident-tabletop-and-present-to-executives.md).** Run the tabletop, keep a decision log, build the communication matrix, and write a one-page executive report.
2. **Final team presentation (this brief).** Present the whole case, Tasks 1–9, as one governance story to a mock Risk Committee.

The tasks are organized for grading. This presentation reorganizes the same work by presenter, in speaking order. Each task belongs to exactly one presenter, so no two people cover the same ground, and each section ends with a spoken bridge into the next.

## Speaking order and ownership

| Order | Presenter | Task(s) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S1 — GRC Coordinator | T1 | Case framing, executive audit summary, overall opinion. Opens the session. |
| 2 | S2 — Asset & Control Analyst | T2, T3 | Incident timeline and stakeholders (T2); asset inventory and control-scope definition, including the MFA and vulnerability-management scope statements (T3). |
| 3 | S3 — Risk Analyst | T4, T5 | All six risk statements as one set (T4); likelihood × impact scoring, top-3 prioritization, and the auditor-challenge outcomes (T5). |
| 4 | S4 — Compliance & Controls Analyst | T6, T7 (remediation) | Framework mapping to NIST CSF, FFIEC, GLBA and OCC (T6); the remediation half of T7: 30-60-90 day actions, exception register, RACI. |
| 5 | S5 — Incident & Communications Analyst | T7 (incident/comms), T8 | The incident and communications half of T7: escalation ladder and notification protocol; the KRI/KPI monitoring dashboard (T8). |
| 6 | Capstone lead (any team member) | T9 | Tabletop decision log, communication matrix, one-page executive report, and a 5-minute Risk Committee briefing. |
| 7 | S6 — Internal Auditor | Cross-task | Closes with combined audit findings: conformance by task, key challenge-log outcomes, and the final scorecard. Findings only, not task content. |

With fewer than six students, combine roles but keep the Internal Auditor separate from the work they review.

## Spoken bridges between sections

- **S1 → S2:** "With the incident framed, here's who should have caught it and what they'd have needed to see."
- **S2 → S3:** "Those control and inventory gaps translate directly into scored risk. Here's the register."
- **S3 → S4:** "Once risk is scored, the next question is what it means against the frameworks we're actually held to."
- **S4 → S5:** "None of this stays fixed without ongoing monitoring and a clear escalation path when it breaks."
- **S5 → Capstone lead:** "So how would this actually play out in the room? Here's the tabletop."
- **Capstone lead → Auditor:** "That's what the team built. Now, independently, here's whether it holds up."

## Why the presentation is structured this way

**T4 stays whole under the Risk Analyst.** Splitting the six risk statements across two presenters is exactly the kind of overlap that breaks a live handoff. S3 owns the full risk narrative and credits other roles' input verbally rather than dividing slides.

**T6 goes to the Compliance & Controls Analyst.** Framework mapping is a direct extension of that role's mandate. T9 needs more original synthesis, so it suits whoever is leading the capstone.

**The Internal Auditor presents last and separately.** The auditor's role is independent challenge, not narration. Presenting combined findings after all task content keeps the challenge visibly independent of the claims being challenged.

## Timing

Plan for 35–45 minutes: about 5 minutes per presenter, the 5-minute Risk Committee briefing inside the T9 slot, and 5–10 minutes for committee questions.

## How it's graded

The presentation is scored with the [final grading rubric](../rubric.md). Executive communication counts most here: clear business language, concise recommendations, and an explicit statement of the decision the committee needs to make.
