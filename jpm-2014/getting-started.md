# Getting Started

This workbook breaks the case into nine guided tasks. Complete the tasks in order. Each task builds a skill that is used in the next task. Do not use the instructor answer key while completing the assignment.

**How this fits with your team's workflow:** this workbook and the task sheets define *what* to produce and why. Your team's shared task tracker (any board or checklist) defines *who* does each part, in what order, and when it is ready for review. Check the tracker for your role and task status; check the task sheet for what your deliverable must contain.

- Use only facts stated in the case unless you clearly label an assumption.
- Write in plain language. Explain technical terms when you use them.
- Every action must have an owner, a deadline and evidence of completion.
- The final submission should tell management what happened, why it happened, what risk remains and what decision is needed.

> **Recommended duration**
>
> Five weeks: two tasks a week in Weeks 1–4, then the capstone (Task 9 and the final team presentation) in Week 5. About 13–15 hours of classroom time plus independent work. The instructor releases each week's two tasks together.

## Suggested six-student role model


| **Student** | **Role** | **Primary responsibilities** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GRC Coordinator | Organizes work, consolidates submissions, manages actions and prepares executive reporting. |
| 2 | Asset and Control Analyst | Owns inventory, control scope, MFA and network-control analysis. |
| 3 | Risk Analyst | Writes risk statements, scores risks and maintains the risk register. |
| 4 | Compliance and Controls Analyst | Maps requirements, identifies evidence and assesses compliance gaps. |
| 5 | Incident and Communications Analyst | Builds the timeline, tabletop decisions and stakeholder communication. |
| 6 | Internal Auditor / Quality Reviewer | Challenges evidence, scoring, ownership and closure; preserves independence. |

> **For smaller classes**
>
> One student can complete the simulation individually, or teams of three can combine roles 1+5, 2+3 and 4+6. For larger classes, create several teams and compare their risk decisions.

## How each week flows

Every week follows the same three-stage pattern across two meetings. Learn it once.

### Stage 1 — Meeting 1 (whole group, about 20 minutes)

The week's two tasks are released. Confirm who is doing which role for each task, and read the relevant case material together if needed. No deliverable work happens yet.

### Stage 2 — Independent work (no waiting)

These four roles can start immediately after Meeting 1. None of them needs another role's output first.

| Role | Works on |
|---|---|
| **S2** Asset & Control Analyst | Their part of both tasks, independently from the case facts |
| **S3** Risk Analyst | Their part of both tasks, independently from the case facts |
| **S4** Compliance & Controls Analyst | Their part of both tasks, independently from the case facts |
| **S5** Incident & Communications Analyst | Their part of both tasks, independently from the case facts |

**Rule: if you are S2, S3, S4 or S5, you never wait on a teammate to start your part of a task.**

### Stage 3 — Before and at Meeting 2 (sequential)

| Role | Step |
|---|---|
| **S1** Coordinator | Consolidates S2–S5's work into one team draft per task. Can't start until they've submitted. |
| **S6** Internal Auditor | Independently reviews each consolidated draft and challenges weak points. Can't start until S1's draft exists. |
| **S1** Coordinator | Submits both approved deliverables for instructor review once S6 signs off. |

**Rule: S1's consolidation and S6's review are the only steps that must happen in order, and only after S2–S5 are done.**

### Target pace

One week = two tasks = two meetings. Because each week carries two tasks, get S2–S5's independent pieces in by mid-week so S1 and S6 have time to consolidate and review both before Meeting 2. If a task is still open after Meeting 2, the usual cause is independent work arriving late.

### Deliverables vs. working inputs

Each task has one group deliverable, and that is what gets graded. The six S1–S6 contributions are working inputs that feed it; they are not graded separately.


## Role assignment sheet

> **How the team works**
>
> Each student owns a distinct role, but the team produces coordinated deliverables. The GRC Coordinator combines the work. The Internal Auditor reviews and challenges the work but should not complete the other students' deliverables.

| **Student** | **Assigned role** | **Primary responsibilities** | **Individual deliverables** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student 1 | GRC Coordinator | Organizes the team, sets deadlines, tracks actions, consolidates submissions and prepares executive reporting. | Project schedule, action tracker, combined submission and executive summary. |
| Student 2 | Asset and Control Analyst | Reviews the asset inventory, defines control scope and analyzes MFA, vulnerability management and network controls. | Asset inventory, control-scope statement and control-gap analysis. |
| Student 3 | Risk Analyst | Writes risk statements, scores risks, maintains the risk register and recommends priorities. | Risk statements, scored risk register and priority rationale. |
| Student 4 | Compliance and Controls Analyst | Maps gaps to NIST CSF and financial-sector expectations, identifies evidence and assesses compliance. | Framework mapping, evidence-request list and compliance conclusion. |
| Student 5 | Incident and Communications Analyst | Builds the incident timeline, identifies stakeholders, records tabletop decisions and prepares communications. | Timeline, stakeholder map, decision log and communication matrix. |
| Student 6 | Internal Auditor / Quality Reviewer | Independently challenges evidence, scoring, ownership and closure. Confirms whether deliverables are complete and defensible. | Quality checklist, challenge notes, validation comments and closure recommendation. |

### Individual Questions Every Student Must Answer

- What is my role in this simulation?
- What decisions can I make or recommend?
- What information do I need from the other roles?
- What deliverables do I own?
- Who must review or approve my work?
- What could go wrong if my responsibility is unclear?

### Team Working Rules

- Use facts from the case and clearly label assumptions.
- Do not change another student's conclusion without discussing the evidence.
- Record unresolved disagreements and ask the Internal Auditor to challenge them.
- Every action must have an owner, target date and expected evidence.
- The final team submission must be internally consistent.
